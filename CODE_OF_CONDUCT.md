# Code of conduct

Working Patterns depends on disagreement. The standard is therefore not “be agreeable”; it is **make disagreement usable**.

Contributors are expected to:

- criticise claims, methods, classifications, and evidence without degrading people;
- distinguish observed behaviour from speculation about motives;
- make conflicts of interest and relevant positionality visible where they affect interpretation;
- avoid publishing unnecessary personal or sensitive information;
- preserve counterevidence even when it is inconvenient to the project;
- accept correction when a source does not support the attached claim;
- avoid using research-status labels as social status labels for contributors or communities.

Harassment, threats, discriminatory abuse, deliberate doxxing, and sustained personal attacks are incompatible with participation.

A strong contribution may say “this conclusion is unsupported” or “this method failed here.” That is not incivility. The project should protect critical friction while refusing personal harm.
