# Research governance

Working Patterns is an open Pyragogy research project. This document defines how the corpus should change as the project grows.

## Authority

Maintainers may merge changes, but **maintainer authority does not convert an interpretation into evidence**.

Research status changes should be justified by inspectable changes in claims, sources, cases, or methodology.

## Persistent IDs

Published identifiers are not silently recycled.

If a pattern is split, merged, narrowed, or retired, the previous ID remains traceable through a revision record.

## Promotion rule

A pattern does not become `corroborated` because:

- many practitioners repeat it;
- several pages cite one another;
- an AI system finds it plausible;
- it appears in multiple derivative traditions;
- the project maintainers prefer it.

Promotion requires evidence appropriate to the specific intervention/outcome and an explicit assessment of limitations and dependence.

## Contestation rule

Counterevidence is retained even when the dominant interpretation survives.

A contributor does not need to replace the project’s conclusion for their evidence to belong in the corpus.

## AI contributions

AI may assist with search, extraction, normalisation, comparison, code, or drafting. AI-generated analysis is not itself empirical evidence.

Material claims introduced through AI assistance must retain source provenance and verification state.

## Conflict of interest

Where a source evaluates a method, product, organisation, or governance framework with which the author is materially connected, that relationship should be recorded when known. It does not automatically invalidate the source; it affects interpretation.

## Future group-submitted evidence

If groups later contribute local experiments or incident reports, submission design should minimise unnecessary personal data and distinguish:

- public organisational facts;
- participant reports;
- researcher interpretation;
- sensitive or identifying material that should not be published.

Open research does not require maximal exposure.
