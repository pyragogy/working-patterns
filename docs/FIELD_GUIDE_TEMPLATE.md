# Field-guide pattern template

This is the proposed bridge from research corpus to a future handbook. It is an editorial view, not the canonical evidence store.

---

# Pattern name

**One sentence:** What the intervention changes, without promising an outcome it has not earned.

## The problem

Describe the observable organisational problem and who bears its consequences.

Avoid abstractions such as “lack of collaboration” when a more measurable problem exists.

## The intervention

Describe what people/agents actually do differently.

If the intervention contains separable components, name them.

## Why it might work

State the proposed mechanism.

Label its status:

`proposed` · `plausible` · `evidenced` · `contested`

## Evidence at a glance

**Current maturity:** …  
**Evidence scope:** …

Summarise the strongest direct evidence, not the largest number of citations.

Then state what that evidence does **not** establish.

## Where it has been observed

List relevant units/contexts and implementation differences.

Do not turn observed sample ranges into recommended thresholds.

## What can go wrong

Separate:

- **Observed** failure modes
- **Reported** failure modes
- **Inferred** failure modes
- **Hypothetical** failure modes

## Boundary conditions

What seems to change transferability or outcome?

Mark which conditions are observed and which remain research hypotheses.

## Alternatives

List other interventions addressing the same problem. Explain which trade-offs would matter when choosing among them.

## Try it as an experiment

When transfer is uncertain, propose a bounded and reversible local test:

- context/decision class;
- duration;
- expected outcome;
- baseline/comparator;
- costs to record;
- subgroup effects;
- stop condition;
- review point.

## What would change our assessment?

Name the missing evidence that could promote, narrow, contest, or retire the pattern.

## Evidence trail

Link claim IDs, cases, studies, and sources rather than presenting a decorative bibliography.

---

The handbook should be easier to read than the research corpus but **never stronger in its claims**.
