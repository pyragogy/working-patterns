# Example consultation — remote team excluded by synchronous decisions

This example demonstrates how Working Patterns can answer a practical question **without pretending the current corpus supports a universal recommendation**.

## Query

> We are a remote team of 14 people. Synchronous decisions exclude several members. Which patterns are relevant, what evidence supports them, under which conditions, and what could go wrong?

## 1. Problem interpretation

Reported problem: participation/access is constrained by co-presence.

What is **not** yet known:

- which decisions are affected;
- why members are excluded (time zone, care work, disability/access, language, shift pattern, status, other);
- whether excluded members need consultation, objection rights, or final authority;
- whether decisions are reversible/urgent;
- current closure rule.

Those differences can change the useful intervention.

## 2. Candidate interventions

### WP-C006-A/B/D — asynchronous proposal, contribution window, decision record

**Why relevant:** removes mandatory co-presence and creates a persistent trace.

**Evidence:** FLOSS archives document real asynchronous decision episodes (`WP-C006-CL01`).

**Limitation:** the available corpus does not causally establish that asynchronous decision-making improves inclusion for a remote team of 14.

**Failure risks:** reading/writing burden, silent non-participation, dominance by members with more time or language fluency, decision effectively made before consultation.

### WP-C005-C — lazy consensus

**Why relevant:** can reduce the number of responses required for some decisions.

**Evidence:** Apache documents a concrete protocol (`WP-C005-CL03`).

**Limitation:** protocol documentation is not an inclusion trial.

**Failure risk:** silence interpreted as informed agreement.

### WP-C005-B — consent

**Why relevant:** gives objections an explicit route into the decision process.

**Evidence:** documented protocol plus an observed sociocratic case (`WP-C005-CL02`, `WP-C005-CL04`).

**Counterevidence/complication:** the same case reports difficult objections and tensions (`WP-C005-CL05`).

### WP-C004 — bounded delegation

**Why relevant:** some decisions may not require all 14 people. Explicit delegation can avoid replacing meeting exclusion with endless asynchronous participation.

**Limitation:** the current corpus documents the problem and implementations more strongly than it isolates effects of mandate duration/revocation.

## 3. A number Working Patterns refuses to misuse

In the FLOSS study, some observed decision episodes involved up to **14 participants**.

That does **not** mean:

> “Asynchronous decision-making works for teams up to 14.”

The 14 is an observed episode size, not an efficacy threshold, recommended team size, or causal moderator.

## 4. Context to obtain before choosing

Ask:

1. What classes of decision are currently made synchronously?
2. Which are reversible?
3. What maximum decision time is acceptable?
4. Who is excluded and why?
5. How much paid/available time does each member have for reading/writing?
6. What languages/access needs matter?
7. Who currently has authority to close a decision?
8. What happens to a late or urgent objection?

## 5. A bounded local experiment

**Hypothesis, not literature result:**

For reversible medium-impact decisions over a four-week window:

1. publish a written proposal;
2. use a clearly stated response window;
3. record who received/read/responded where ethically and practically appropriate;
4. record which contributions changed the proposal;
5. designate a closure rule and responsible closer;
6. publish decision + rationale;
7. allow an explicit escalation path for urgent/high-impact objection.

Measure:

- participation among previously excluded members;
- influence (not just comments): did contributions change decisions?;
- time to decision;
- total reading/writing/synthesis hours;
- late objections/reopened decisions;
- perceived legitimacy;
- burden on the person synthesising/closing.

Then compare with the team's recent synchronous baseline rather than with an imaginary universal standard.

## 6. Current conclusion

Working Patterns can justify **trying a bounded asynchronous procedure as an experiment** given this problem.

It cannot currently justify the stronger claim that asynchronous decision-making is the best or universally more inclusive method for a 14-person remote team.
