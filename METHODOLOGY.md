# Methodology

Working Patterns studies organisational practices as **specific interventions in context**, not as universal best practices.

## Core research question

> Which organisational patterns appear to work, for which problems, under which conditions, according to what evidence — and where do they stop working?

## Unit of evaluation

The central relation is:

**problem → intervention → implementation → outcome → evidence → conditions**

The unit of evaluation is not a broad family such as “consensus”, “care”, or “conflict”. It is a specific intervention, as implemented in a particular context, evaluated against one or more explicit outcomes.

## Classification rules

1. **Decompose packages.** Separate components when the literature does not evaluate the complete package.
2. **Use causal language narrowly.** Reserve causal claims for designs that justify them. Association or mediation alone is not causal identification.
3. **Do not transfer automatically across levels.** Evidence about individuals, small groups, organisations, communities, and networks remains scoped to those units unless transfer is justified.
4. **Track independence.** Record authors, organisations, datasets, cases, and theoretical traditions separately.
5. **Search for counterevidence.** Distinguish evidence against an intervention from evidence against a broad justification for it.
6. **Separate adoption from implementation.** A declared practice does not establish implementation fidelity.
7. **Keep outcomes distinct.** Performance, participation, power distribution, sustainability, learning, legitimacy, and continuity are not synonyms.
8. **Preserve unknowns.** `unknown` does not mean zero, absent, ineffective, or disproven.
9. **Define maturity conservatively.** `documented` means describable and documented, not effective.
10. **Preserve revisions.** When a pattern is narrowed, split, or revised, keep the prior formulation and the reason for change.

## Pattern maturity

- `candidate`
- `documented`
- `corroborated`
- `contested`
- `revised`
- `retired`

Maturity is separate from evidence scope.

## Evidence scope

- `narrow-context`
- `multi-context`
- `cross-domain`

These labels describe the breadth of the evidence available for the specified intervention/outcome. They are not confidence scores.

## Evidence relations

An evidence link connects **one source or study to one claim**.

- `supports`
- `complicates`
- `contradicts`
- `context-only`

A source can support one claim and complicate another. Working Patterns therefore does not assign a single global relation between a source and an entire pattern.

## Verification states used in Research Map 0.2

- `FT` — relevant passage verified in accessible full text; not a reanalysis of underlying data.
- `ABS` — verified in an abstract or editorial preview; methods not fully appraised.
- `PR` — verified in a practitioner document or author account.
- `SYN` — Working Patterns synthesis or methodological inference; not a study result.

Structured records use explicit machine-readable equivalents where practical.

## Adoption and implementation

Where possible, cases distinguish:

- `declared_practice`
- `observed_practice`
- `implementation_fidelity`
- `known_deviations`

An organisation saying it uses consensus, sociocracy, retrospectives, or shared leadership is not sufficient evidence that a specified intervention was implemented.

## Outcomes

At minimum, research should keep the following outcome families separable:

- operational capacity
- decision quality
- participation
- power distribution
- sustainability
- learning
- legitimacy
- continuity

A pattern may improve one outcome while degrading another.

## Costs

Costs should be recorded when evidence permits, including:

- time
- facilitation
- cognitive load
- emotional labour
- coordination overhead
- training
- administration

The relevant question is not only how much a practice costs, but **who bears the cost**.

## Failure modes

Failure modes are labelled as:

- `observed`
- `reported`
- `inferred`
- `hypothetical`

These labels must not be collapsed.

## Source and study are different entities

A source is a document or retrievable research object. A study is the empirical or analytical design reported in a source. Multiple sources can report the same study or dataset; one source can describe more than one study.

This distinction is necessary to avoid false replication counts.

## Genealogy and independence

Convergence is informative but is not automatically independent confirmation. Independence should be assessed across:

- authors
- organisations
- datasets
- cases
- theoretical tradition

For example, Bartlett, Loomio, and Enspiral belong to a connected provenance line; publications reusing the same dataset do not count as independent replications.

## Counterevidence

Counterevidence search is mandatory for a deep dive. Research notes should distinguish:

- direct negative evidence about the intervention;
- evidence complicating a proposed mechanism;
- negative cases with unknown causality;
- absence of direct evaluation.

Preferred wording when appropriate:

> No direct counterevidence identified in the searched corpus.

This must never be paraphrased as “there is no counterevidence”.

## Current review status

Working Patterns is an **exploratory evidence-mapping programme**, not a completed systematic review. The current corpus contains targeted searches, full-text reads where available, abstracts, practitioner material, cases, and synthesis. Search coverage is incomplete and must be represented as such.

## Future machine use

The structured corpus should eventually support evidence-aware retrieval and advisory workflows. A future system must be able to expose evidence, counterevidence, conditions, alternatives, costs, and missing context.

It must not convert the corpus into an automatic ranking of “best practices”.
