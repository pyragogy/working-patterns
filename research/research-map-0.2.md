# Working Patterns — Research Map 0.2

**Deep Evidence Cycle · 14 September 2026**

## Principal result

The method held when the unit of evaluation became:

> **a specific intervention, implemented in a context, relative to a specified outcome**

It did not hold when broad families such as “consensus”, “care”, or “conflict” were assigned one reputation.

This cycle deepened the seven priorities from Research Map 0.1. It did not reopen general field reconnaissance and did not select handbook chapters.

## First assessment of the seven deep dives

| ID | Pattern family | Assessment | Maturity / scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| WP-C001 | Explicit norms and enforcement | Context-limited. Experimental support exists for rule visibility in one moderated environment; this does not validate participatory rule-writing, sanctions, or the entire enforcement system. | `documented` / `narrow-context` |
| WP-C002 | Structured retrospectives / debriefs with follow-up | Structured debrief core is supported by quantitative syntheses; incremental benefit of responsibility/deadline/follow-up remains insufficiently isolated. | C002-A `corroborated` / `multi-context`; package `documented` |
| WP-C003 | Explicit distribution of care work | Practices are documentable and the distributive problem has evidence; direct evaluation of care redistribution remains scarce. | `documented` / `narrow-context` |
| WP-C004 | Informal power and mandates | Making authority explicit is observable, but mandates/elections/rotation are not shown to remove informal power. | `documented` / `narrow-context` |
| WP-C005 | Consensus / consent / lazy consensus | Distinct protocols with strong documentation but insufficient homogeneous causal comparison. | `documented` / `narrow-context` |
| WP-C006 | Asynchronous decision-making | Real distributed practices are observed; causal advantage over synchronous decision-making, especially for inclusion, is uncertain. | `documented` / `narrow-context` |
| WP-C007 | Conflict and dissent | The generic formula “more conflict improves decisions” is not supportable. Task/relationship/process conflict and authentic/assigned dissent must be separated. | `contested`; associations span multiple contexts |

`context-limited` is an assessment phrase, not a new maturity enum.

## Five findings that changed the research

1. **Evidence for a problem does not validate its proposed solution.** Low-promotability-task experiments are not experiments on stewardship or care rotation.
2. **Declared practice can differ from implementation.** Sociocracy and Agile retrospective cases expose this directly.
3. **More papers do not imply independent datasets.** The 2022 Keiser–Arthur synthesis extends/includes the earlier corpus rather than independently replicating it.
4. **Outcomes can move in different directions.** In the rule-visibility study, increased participation can coexist with more removed comments in absolute terms even when compliance improves.
5. **A negative case does not identify causality by itself.** A project that later stops development does not prove its decision procedure caused the cessation.

## Updated research relation

Research Map 0.2 refined the central relation to:

**problem → intervention → implementation → outcome → evidence → conditions**

A source can document a protocol, observe it being used, compare it with an alternative, measure an effect, or propose a mechanism. Those functions remain separate.

## Classification rules adopted

- Decompose packages when evidence evaluates only components.
- Reserve causal language for designs that justify it.
- Do not transfer findings automatically across individuals, small groups, organisations, communities, or networks.
- Track independence across author, organisation, dataset, case, and tradition.
- Distinguish counterevidence to the intervention from evidence against a broad justification.
- Do not infer implementation fidelity from a practice label.
- Keep performance, participation, power, sustainability, learning, legitimacy, and continuity separate.
- Preserve `unknown` as distinct from absence.
- `documented` means describable/documented, not effective.
- Preserve previous formulations when a pattern is narrowed or split.

## Verification vocabulary used in 0.2

- `FT` — relevant passage inspected in accessible full text; not reanalysis of data;
- `ABS` — abstract/editorial preview inspected; methods not fully audited;
- `PR` — practitioner document or author account inspected;
- `SYN` — Working Patterns synthesis/methodological inference.

## Deep dives

### WP-C001 — Explicit norms and enforcement

The broad seed was decomposed into:

- C001-A: display relevant rules at the point of participation;
- C001-B: publish enforcement criteria, responsibility, and procedure;
- C001-C: reporting, sanctions, and review;
- C001-D: member participation in norm definition.

Direct evidence was strongest for C001-A in one online-community field experiment. Punishment research and moderation studies complicate general claims about enforcement and cost but are not negative replications of rule visibility.

Observed/relevant conditions include newcomer status, pre-existing moderation, and the specific digital environment. Language, readability, moderator capacity, legitimacy, and appeal remain transfer questions.

Final: `documented`, `narrow-context`, context-limited.

### WP-C002 — Structured retrospectives / debriefs with follow-up

Components:

- C002-A: structured debrief after activity;
- C002-B: convert reflection into identifiable actions;
- C002-C: assign responsibility and verify later implementation;
- C002-D: specific added review components.

Meta-analytic evidence supports the debrief/AAR core across multiple contexts. It does not establish that every added component improves outcomes. Agile observations document implementation variation, and later synthesis reports some components without significant incremental contribution.

Final: C002-A provisionally `corroborated`, `multi-context`; A+B+C package remains `documented`.

### WP-C003 — Explicit distribution of care work

Components include visibility, negotiable allocation, rotation of transferable tasks, recognition/compensation, and stewardship relationships.

The problem — unequal allocation of low-recognition work — has empirical support. Loomio documents stewardship. The current corpus does not directly establish that the proposed redistribution package produces more equitable care load.

Important boundaries include skill, trust, consent, compensation, gendered expectations, precarity, and real ability to refuse. Equal task counts do not imply equal burden.

Final: `documented`, `narrow-context`.

### WP-C004 — Informal power and mandates

Components include explicit domains, selection/duration/revocation, observability of authority, distribution of skill/information, and rotation.

Research on structurelessness and online-community authority concentration supports the importance of the problem. A sociocratic case documents formal governance while also reporting persistent status asymmetries.

Founder dependence, credentials, rare expertise, succession cost, candidate availability, and retaliation risk remain central conditions.

Final: `documented`; remedy evidence narrower than problem evidence.

### WP-C005 — Consensus / consent / lazy consensus

The cycle refused to treat these as synonyms:

- consensus integrates needs through locally specified agreement/block/stand-aside rules;
- consent examines objections relative to purpose;
- lazy consensus publishes a proposal and acts after an objection window under a specified protocol.

Protocol documentation is strong. Comparative causal evidence across equivalent decision classes/outcomes is weak. The sociocratic case reports both use and difficult objections.

Silence cannot automatically be interpreted as informed agreement.

Final: `documented`, `narrow-context`.

### WP-C006 — Asynchronous decision-making

Components:

- written accessible proposal;
- explicit contribution/objection interval;
- closure rule and responsible closer;
- decision/rationale record;
- escalation to another mode when necessary.

FLOSS archives show real asynchronous decision episodes and variation in closure/process. They do not establish a universal inclusion advantage over synchronous decision-making.

Relevant transfer questions include decision type, reversibility, urgency, literacy, language, access, notification load, paid time, and whether decisions happen privately before public consultation.

Final: `documented`, `narrow-context`, context-limited.

### WP-C007 — Conflict and dissent

Components were separated into:

- collecting objections/alternatives;
- protecting authentic dissent;
- assigned devil's advocate;
- separate process for relational conflict;
- contestable division of work/responsibility.

Meta-analytic evidence contradicts a simple positive “task conflict → performance” story, while relationship/process conflict show more stable negative associations. An experimental literature also suggests that assigned devil's advocacy is not equivalent to authentic dissent.

Final: broad pattern `contested`. Future work must separate protected dissent, objection review, and relational-conflict handling.

No sufficiently documented cooperative dissolution was identified that could responsibly be attributed to one of the seven interventions. Generic organisational failure was not substituted for direct counterevidence.

## Evidence matrix — key reading

- C001: direct experimental evidence exists for rule visibility, not the full enforcement package.
- C002: direct synthesis support exists for structured debrief; follow-up is less isolated.
- C003: problem evidence is stronger than intervention evidence.
- C004: problem evidence is stronger than mandate/rotation effects.
- C005: protocols and cases exist; homogeneous comparisons are missing.
- C006: real episodes are observable; direct synchronous comparator is missing.
- C007: intervention research and conflict-state meta-analysis must not be conflated.

## Genealogy findings

Important dependency relationships:

- Bartlett ↔ Loomio/Enspiral: connected practitioner line;
- AAR ↔ debrief ↔ retrospective: overlapping components, not lexical equivalence;
- Keiser–Arthur 2021 → 2022: shared/extended synthesis corpus;
- classical sociocracy ↔ S3: connected family, implementations not interchangeable;
- Apache lazy consensus ↔ asynchronous mode: protocol contains temporal structure; not synonyms;
- Freeman ↔ oligarchy research: convergence on the problem, not empirical convergence on a remedy;
- minority dissent ↔ team conflict: partially intersecting constructs.

This prevents terminology diffusion from being counted as replication.

## Source-quality lesson

Full-text access and causal quality are separate dimensions. Some full texts are observational or practitioner material; some important syntheses were only available through abstracts/previews in this cycle.

Meta-analysis audit also requires inclusion criteria, heterogeneity, publication bias, dataset overlap, and construct equivalence rather than citing the pooled effect alone.

## Licensing lesson

The updated registry included verified CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC, Apache 2.0, partial verification, and unknown/no-open-commercial-licence states.

Unknown rights are not treated as permission to reuse.

## Methodological problems encountered

1. Packages hide the actual treatment.
2. Problems are often studied better than solutions.
3. Proxies can change the meaning of outcomes.
4. Absence in traces is ambiguous.
5. Studies more easily observe people who remain than people who leave.
6. Costs are distributed, not merely summed.
7. Publications can create false independence.
8. Access does not guarantee causal quality.
9. Historical genealogy remains incomplete.
10. Mutable web sources need stronger future preservation/versioning.

## Priority research gaps after 0.2

- independent replication of C001-A;
- incremental effect of follow-up after debrief;
- actual redistribution of care workload;
- mandates versus effective power;
- homogeneous comparison of decision protocols;
- participation **and influence** in asynchronous decisions;
- personal consequences of dissent;
- leavers, abandoned practices, and dissolved groups;
- total/distributed cost;
- updated reviews;
- low-resource, multilingual, cooperative, and mutual-aid contexts.

## Recommended next deep dives

1. Follow-up that actually changes work after retrospectives.
2. Care allocation: rotation vs specialisation vs compensation.
3. Silence and closure in asynchronous decisions.
4. Mandates during succession/revocation.
5. Consent under persistent objection.
6. Protection of authentic dissent and consequences for dissenters.

Before further promotion of C002-A, full-text auditing and study deduplication should be strengthened.

## MCP readiness result

The 0.2 corpus can already support an **argued consultation** for a question such as:

> We are a remote team of 14 people. Synchronous decisions exclude several members. Which patterns are relevant, what evidence supports them, under which conditions, and what could go wrong?

It can retrieve plausible options and expose evidence, limitations, and risks. It **cannot** responsibly infer that asynchronous decision-making “works for teams up to 14” merely because 14 appears as an episode-participant maximum in a FLOSS study.

Before local recommendation, it would need decision type/reversibility, timing constraints, who is excluded and why, language/access, reading time, current authority, and urgent-objection handling.

### Readiness

- retrieve patterns by problem: conceptually ready;
- filter by context/scale: partial;
- return claim/source/locator: available in the current registry;
- expose counterevidence/limits: available with uneven coverage;
- distinguish adoption/implementation: model supports it, cases partially populated;
- avoid double-counting: partial; major dependencies identified;
- causally recommend “the best pattern”: **not supported**;
- persistent/versioned corpus: repository work now addresses this gap;
- unsupervised automated recommendation: premature.

## 0.2 conclusion

Working Patterns currently supports:

> **an evidence-aware, argued consultation — not automatic selection of best practices.**

Its most useful function is making visible the difference between documentation, observation, empirical support, counterevidence, and residual ignorance.
