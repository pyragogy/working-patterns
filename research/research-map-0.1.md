# Working Patterns — Research Map 0.1

**Field reconnaissance · 14 September 2026**

> Which organisational patterns appear to work, for which problems, under which conditions, according to what evidence — and where do they stop working?

## Purpose

The first research cycle established whether a serious Working Patterns project was feasible. It mapped the field, extracted the initial seed corpus, created a provisional problem taxonomy, tested an evidence relation vocabulary, and drafted the first candidate-pattern records.

It was **not** a systematic review and did not attempt to extract the eventual 200–400 candidate-pattern discovery pool.

## Operational model

Every evaluation should eventually distinguish:

**problem → intervention → proposed mechanism → context → observed outcomes → costs/consequences → alternatives**

“The group works” is not an adequate outcome. The initial outcome families were:

- operational capacity;
- decision quality;
- power distribution;
- participation;
- sustainability;
- learning;
- legitimacy;
- continuity.

These can move in different directions. A practice can accelerate execution while concentrating power, or increase participation while shifting labour onto moderators.

## Provisional problem taxonomy

The initial twenty tags were grouped into eleven areas:

| Area | Core tags | Organisational question |
|---|---|---|
| Authority and influence | power, leadership | Who exercises power, under what mandate? |
| Decision and dissent | decision-making, dissent | Who decides and how can a decision be challenged? |
| Conflict and harm | conflict | How are disagreement, violation, and abuse distinguished? |

| Coordination of work | coordination, division of labour | How are tasks and dependencies managed? |
| Accountability | accountability | Who owes what to whom? |
| Care and capacity | care | Who sustains the group, with which resources? |
| Communication and knowledge | communication, information, organisational memory | Who knows what and how does it persist? |
| Governance architecture | governance, autonomy | Where are decision rights located? |

Three cross-cutting dimensions were added as hypotheses for later verification: material resources, accessibility, and external dependencies.

## First evidence map

The reconnaissance already showed why claim-level relations were necessary.

Examples:

- Cox, Arnold, and Villamayor Tomás (2010) provide empirical support for Ostrom-related design principles in natural-resource commons, not universal transfer to all groups.
- Tannenbaum and Cerasoli (2013) support the broad debrief family, not every retrospective format.
- Frazier et al. (2017) provide psychological-safety context but do not validate a specific check-in/retreat intervention.
- Babcock et al. (2017) support the problem of unequal low-promotability task allocation, not the effectiveness of care stewardship.
- Shaw and Hill (2014) complicate the assumption that open collaboration automatically creates democratic governance.
- Matias (2019) provides a bounded experimental test of rule visibility in r/science and also reveals possible moderation trade-offs.

This produced the initial relation vocabulary:

- `supports`
- `complicates`
- `contradicts`
- `context-only`

A relation belongs to a **claim**, not globally to a source.

## First candidate-pattern tests

Three records were used to test the emerging format:

### WP-C001 — Explicit behavioural agreements

Status at 0.1: `documented`.

The seed describes explicit norms/boundaries; a Matias experiment supports one narrower component — making existing rules visible — in one moderated online setting. Co-design, enforcement, review, and broader portability remained unestablished.

### WP-C002 — Structured reflection with a follow-up change

Status at 0.1: `documented`.

The seed proposes review plus change. Debrief meta-analysis was identified as relevant adjacent evidence, but equivalence between military/training debriefs, Agile retrospectives, and the seed format was not assumed.

### WP-C003 — Explicit allocation of care work

Status at 0.1: `documented`.

Practitioner material documented stewardship. Experimental research supported the existence of unequal low-promotability task allocation but did not directly test redistribution of care work.

## Overlap problems identified

Normalisation could not be lexical. The first cycle identified distinctions such as:

- retrospective vs debrief vs after-action review: overlapping functions, not necessarily identical treatments;
- explicit norms vs constitutions/governance rules: different levels;
- asynchronous decision-making vs lazy consensus: temporal mode vs authorisation rule;
- consensus vs consent vs lazy consensus: related but different objection/closure procedures;
- stewardship vs mentoring vs peer review: relational overlap with distinct purposes;
- talking about power vs rotation vs election: same problem family, different interventions;
- cadence vs meeting type: frequency does not define the intervention;
- transparency vs documentation vs organisational memory: publishing does not guarantee retrieval, comprehension, or maintenance.

## Genealogy and false convergence

The cycle established that the project must track independence separately across:

- authors;
- organisations;
- datasets;
- cases;
- theoretical traditions.

Sociocracy 3.0 explicitly synthesises sociocracy, Agile, and Lean influences; those names cannot be counted as three independent discoveries of the same pattern. Governing Knowledge Commons is intellectually linked to Ostrom rather than an independent origin.

## Licensing discipline

The research encountered CC0, CC BY-SA, Apache 2.0, CC BY-NC-SA, and unknown/unverified statuses.

Four editorial destinations were therefore separated:

1. research notes and interpretations;
2. verbatim quotations with locator;
3. reusable licensed material with conditions;
4. original Working Patterns prose.

Free access, downloadable PDFs, and commercial republication permission are separate facts.

## Priority gaps

The most important gaps identified were:

- failed/dissolved groups and groups no longer visible to practitioner networks;
- people who leave or do not participate;
- material/time resources enabling a practice;
- difference between declared rules and actual implementation;
- selection effects among adopters;
- duration of effects;
- scale and composition;
- pattern interaction;
- founder dependence;
- exit, split, and closure as outcomes rather than simply failures;
- geographic and linguistic bias;
- outdated reviews requiring update.

> **“No counterevidence acquired” must never become “no counterevidence exists.”**

## Deep-dive priorities generated by 0.1

High priority:

- explicit norms and enforcement;
- retrospectives with follow-up;
- distribution of care;
- informal power and mandates;
- consensus / consent / lazy consensus;
- asynchronous decisions;
- conflict and dissent.

Medium priority:

- delegation with explicit domains;
- onboarding;
- organisational memory and traceability;
- monitoring and response to violations;
- succession, exit, and split.

## 0.1 conclusion

The field contains real links between organisational practices and research, but those links often support **only one component, one problem, one mechanism, or one context**.

Working Patterns must preserve that granularity rather than convert proximity into proof.
