# Licensing policy

Working Patterns studies material with heterogeneous rights and licences. **No blanket repository-wide licence is currently asserted over third-party material.**

This file is a research and editorial policy, not legal advice.

## Four material classes

Every extraction should keep these categories distinct:

1. **Research notes** — bibliographic facts, structured claims, classifications, limitations, and original analytical notes.
2. **Quotations** — verbatim third-party text, kept short, attributed, and locator-specific.
3. **Reusable licensed material** — third-party material whose specific version has a licence that permits the intended reuse, with conditions recorded.
4. **Original Working Patterns prose** — project-authored definitions, synthesis, methodology, and editorial text.

Free access, downloadable access, open access, and permission to republish commercially are different properties.

## Source-level licence registry

Licence status belongs to the **specific source/version**, not automatically to a domain, publisher, author, or research tradition.

Current research includes material identified as CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC, Apache-licensed, and material for which no open commercial reuse licence has yet been verified.

Unknown licence status means **do not presume reusable**.

## Seed Corpus 001

Richard D. Bartlett's *Patterns for Decentralised Organising* declares CC0 1.0 Universal in its repository. Working Patterns keeps its provenance even though attribution is not required by CC0.

## Share-alike and non-commercial sources

Where a source uses BY-SA or NC terms, the project records those conditions and avoids silently incorporating protected/adapted material into a differently licensed commercial product.

Knowledge derived from research, original synthesis, quotations, and adapted source material must remain distinguishable in the editorial pipeline.

## Future handbook

No licence for a future handbook is implied by this repository. The handbook's original prose, quotations, licensed adaptations, datasets, and software may require different rights treatment.

## Future code

If substantial software is added later (for example an MCP server), code licensing should be decided separately from research-content licensing.
